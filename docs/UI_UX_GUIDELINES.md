# DeltaMon UI/UX guidelines

Read this before every UI, UX, copy, or frontend change. Keep it current when design decisions change. The product concept and staged implementation live in [BALANCE_ENGINE_PLAN.md](BALANCE_ENGINE_PLAN.md).

## Product promise

DeltaMon pairs an approachable printmaker character with a precision instrument for understanding a vault. Help a first-time visitor answer: What do I own? What is the vault doing? How current is the hedge measurement? What happens when I act?

- Show actual holdings and manager-reported short notional as separate measurements. Capital backing a short is not the short's size.
- Never claim measured balance from missing, stale, future-dated, or illustrative data. The ±2% target is a measured UI band, not a guarantee.
- Label examples as illustrative. State the manager-report trust boundary near the relevant data.
- Explain risk and exit paths in plain language.
- Keep consumer copy focused on shares, the MON position, the reported short, report freshness, and exit liquidity. Do not explain the API or contract limitation behind the separate trading account in the public journey. Use protocol role names such as "perp manager" only in operator controls where they identify an actual permission.
- State the material custody and reporting boundary beside the live position: funds backing the short leave the vault's direct control, and the separate account's report can be delayed or inaccurate. Avoid repeating that warning in every deposit step.
- Keep the standalone How it works and Risks sections hidden for now; they do not add enough value to the current public page. Do not show links to those sections while they are hidden.
- Describe the short as automated only when that matches the deployed operating state. A deposit confirmation means shares were minted; it does not mean allocation or hedging has completed.
- Use literal labels for money actions and exits. Keep event or hackathon credits out of the consumer footer. Label simple annualized staking and funding estimates as estimates, not APY, and make the current performance fee rate visible before a deposit and beside redemption.
- Historical charts distinguish actual Perpl funding and fill fees, recorded contract share value, and estimated MON staking income. The staking estimate uses the recorded staked quantity and assumed APY; never add virtual rewards to contract NAV. Missing observation intervals stay visible as gaps. Show capital composition using holdings and booked short equity, including idle AUSD where present; short notional is exposure, and accrued fees are a separate liability. See [ACCOUNTING.md](ACCOUNTING.md).
- Lead with concrete holdings and reported short data. Use printmaking as the visual connection to the deposit receipt; do not make a first-time visitor decode a metaphor in a headline or tagline.
- Describe Strategy 01 as MON staking in the hero. Keep the live exposure reading labeled "MON held" because it includes staked, idle, and unstaking MON; do not present the total as the staked balance.
- Keep the hero headline to two lines on desktop: "MON staked." then "Short reported." Scale the second line within the headline so the art and introductory copy have room; preserve the same two-part reading on mobile.

## Current visual language: The Printmaker / Balance Engine

- Warm cream carries the hero and real-position chapter in light mode, with a fully designed dark mode through the visible theme switch. A compact hedge-status strip, performance charts, and clear source labels distinguish measured vault data from the illustrative character world without a full-width color break.
- Use the CSS tokens in `apps/fe/src/app/globals.css`: ink plum, warm cream, MON violet, muted amber for the short leg, and green or coral only for status. Keep contrast strong on both themes.
- Bricolage Grotesque is the bold display face; IBM Plex Sans is body copy; IBM Plex Mono is for measurements and small labels. Use tabular figures for live numbers.
- The public home page carries a restrained **printed publication** treatment from introduction to receipt: fine masthead rules, flat square actions, relaxed body copy, and a monospaced basics rail. Hedge status and the Field record use warm paper sheets, subtle shadows, aligned ledgers and dashed internal separators; period and funding selections use a violet underline with a quiet tint. Retain the character, chapter layout, chart colors, status semantics and all source disclosures. Reserve torn edges, vault stamps and barcodes for deposit and return receipts. Finish with a compact publication imprint and clearly linked resources. Use matching dark paper and visible keyboard focus; these home-page refinements are scoped separately from the operator console.
- The site header is an editorial **Strategy 01 masthead**: a printed registration rail, a small decorative two-sided beam, the DeltaMon stamp and wordmark, and numbered links to the real position, deposit, and console. Keep the beam decorative and free of live-status meaning. Keep all three links visible on small screens and the wallet entry distinct from navigation.
- The hero character is now **the printmaker**: the recognizable violet silhouette rendered in satin violet and plum, holding a brass-handled ink roller and inspecting a cream proof sheet. Use the transparent artwork directly on the page with no pink orbit, weights or counterweight labels. The static illustration introduces the Deposit Press's materials and craft; the proof is decorative and carries no balance or confirmation claim. The header uses a simplified vector printmaker mark with a proof sheet and roller, kept crisp at mobile sizes. Keep the public name generic; the earlier Tippet counterweight story remains an optional legacy exploration behind the lore flag.
- **Tippet**, the little counterweight, is a **brand guide**, not the keeper, a guarantee of balance, or a transaction status indicator. Tippet's recurring question is “What's on the other side?” and its follow-up is “When was it measured?” Its silhouette and personality must remain useful when future strategies involve other assets and hedge methods. Today's MON vault is labeled Strategy 01; do not imply other strategies exist until they do.
- Tippet's origin and the DeltaMon Field Book are clearly brand fiction. Do not present them as the founder story, product history, or evidence of any live position. See [counterweight-system.md](design-explorations/deltamon-2026/counterweight-system.md) for the character bible and chapter rules.
- Keep Tippet's name, origin, personality copy, and Field Book off the public site by default. `NEXT_PUBLIC_TIPPET_LORE_ENABLED=true` is an explicit build-time preview switch; leave it unset or `false` for end users. The printmaker illustration and strategy explanation remain visible without the lore.
- Use the character for introductions, explanations, and optional verified success moments. Never make it celebrate a pending transaction, an unverified hedge, or a yield estimate. Keep operational copy direct and serious.
- The real-position chapter uses a compact **Hedge status** strip above performance. Show “MON held”, “Reported MON short”, and “Net MON exposure”; compare the first two with thin bars on the same USD scale. Source, status, and report age stay visible. Render a net value only when current report data supports it; stale short readings retain their last reported number with an unverified label and no filled bar. Label illustrative values as examples, and treat future-dated report times as invalid. Put vault value, share price, and deposit cap in a slim metadata rail rather than a separate large stats ledger. Keep the separate-account custody and reporting boundary immediately below the readings. Capital backing the short belongs in the composition chart; earnings belong in daily income. Do not repeat them in a large exposure graphic or annualized yield strip. The earlier main-page Exposure Plate exploration is retained in [position-section-research.md](design-explorations/deltamon-2026/position-section-research.md); its arc illustration remains only in the explanatory FAQ.
- Below Hedge status, the **Field record** presents performance in the same cream-and-plum chapter: a large “Cumulative vault return” chart in percent beside a composition ring and selectable asset ledger, with daily staking/funding bars below. Label the supporting caption “Measured by change in value per sdMON share.” The headline measures actual change in recorded contract value per sdMON share within the selected 7D/30D window, using the first valid share value as the baseline; display starting/latest recorded values. This is vault-period performance, not a wallet's personal return. Share return includes contract-valued holdings, reported short equity and accrued fee liabilities; staking rewards enter share value when claimed. Display the short-book timestamp and explain that asynchronous MON prices and short re-marks can move the reading; stale reports suppress return. Keep missing dates and empty-vault return segments disconnected, display one recorded day as a point, and hatch partial daily income. Funding and estimated staking remain supporting income data in USD; never add virtual staking rewards to NAV. Income subtotals sum only days with both sources, disclose partial coverage and the latest staking APY assumption, and exclude price PnL and fees. The Observed/Settled switch applies only to daily funding. Observed includes changes in unpaid accrual; Settled shows actual venue payments. Combine idle USDC and AUSD into one “USD” slice and ledger row using summed recorded USD values; show accrued fees outside the gross-asset ring. Expose record times, tiny signed USD amounts, coverage, and source notes. Keep the ring and ledger visible; use a disclosure for accounting explanation. Hovering a ring slice shows asset name, USD value, and percentage of gross assets, temporarily highlighting that slice; leaving restores any ledger selection. Stack charts on phones and preserve keyboard date inspection and reduced-motion behavior.
- The 60/40 FAQ uses a compact Exposure Plate with the same arc geometry and explicit example labels. Its $60 long and $60 short illustrate equal notional exposure; $40 appears in a separate backing-capital compartment. Never show the legacy circular dial for this explanation.
- Prefer graphic print textures, irregular illustration edges, and bold typography in storytelling. Keep wallet actions and financial data in calm, stable surfaces with visible focus and minimal decorative motion.
- The home-page deposit chapter uses the **Deposit Press** selected from `/deposit-concepts`: a cream-and-violet mechanical press beside “Ready when you are”, followed by a wide paper ticket with a dark top rail, indexed progress and directional content transitions. Source, amount and review pair a short introduction with the active controls; Track brings the machine beside the literal confirmation list. Keep the wallet, quote, allowance, deposit, recovery and transaction-link behavior intact. Share the renderer and transition components with the concept preview, load Three.js near the deposit chapter, and keep all simulation controls and fictional balances off the home page. Apply the site's light/dark theme, expose pause, honor reduced motion and pause hidden/offscreen illustration.
- The Entry Ticket is a **one-face-at-a-time sequence**, not an expanding form. Keep the compact registration rail and indexed progress rail fixed while access, source (when multiple chains are available), amount, review, and transaction tracking replace one another in the same area. Selecting a source advances immediately. Back and retry actions preserve the source and amount whenever they remain valid; an irreversible wallet action begins only from review.
- The home-page source step uses readable network rows with real USDC balances. Selecting a funded network advances immediately and carries its name through review and tracking. Show unreadable balances as unavailable and disable their selection; retain a fundable zero-balance Monad option. The development lab retains the earlier constellation for comparison.
- Live tracking uses the **progressive printed receipt**. Only a contiguous sequence of confirmed milestones advances the paper; pending rollers never imply elapsed-time progress. Direct Monad deposits distinguish wallet signing, allowance, vault receipt and shares; a successful approval cannot finish the receipt. Cross-chain deposits preserve source, route, vault and verified-share milestones, and Aurora success alone cannot stamp the final record. Failures hold the partial receipt with recovery controls available. Restored receipts settle immediately. The development lab retains the earlier transfer map.
- Deposit tracking includes a **strategy branch** from the vault: “Put funds to work” pairs “Buy & stake MON” in violet with “Increase the MON short” in amber. Keep the four verified deposit milestones; receiving USDC and minting sdMON happen together, while allocation is separate. Label the branch “Vault strategy / illustration” and keep its explanation visible at every transaction state. A finite illustration plays automatically once after a freshly initiated live deposit has a verified share mint; restored receipts do not autoplay, and the lab stays manual unless “Preview live autoplay” is enabled. Refresh the position immediately, then reveal the return route after the illustration ends; reduced motion skips the illustration and reveals the return route immediately. Manual replay remains available. The sequence begins at the vault node, travels down its connector, then splits into the violet staking and amber short routes before their illustrations respond; it is never a live allocation signal, fifth milestone, or hedge confirmation. Transfer motion uses a single traveling signal, a drawn receipt path, and a settling checkmark. Keep station labels fixed and readable on phones. Build the connector and fork in the same measured SVG coordinate space; avoid stretching a fixed fork with non-scaling strokes, which changes normalized pulse travel at different widths. Provide pause/resume, honor reduced motion, and pause ambient animation offscreen or in a hidden tab. Existing confirmed receipts render in their settled state without replaying success.
- The return route continues the Deposit Press as a **position receipt**: use the same warm paper, plum ink and violet accents, a fine registration rail, subtle torn edges, a decorative vault stamp and barcode, and dashed separators. Lead with “Your share. Your next move.” Present shares, redeemable shares and cost basis as a printed ledger; pair the redemption explanation with a simple underlined amount field and flat action button. Preserve all live readings, liquidity and fee disclosures, transaction feedback, and the existing post-confirmation scroll/focus target. The stamp identifies the vault, not a successful redemption. Use a corresponding dark-paper palette and stack the form on phones. Keep redeemable liquidity and exit feedback more prominent than the decoration.
- After a fresh deposit is confirmed by a vault receipt or share mint, refresh the position and guide the visitor to **The return route**. Keep the deposit receipt available above. Do not advance or scroll on approval, submission, a pending route, or a restored historical session.
- The vault console uses an **operator's field plate**: a configured-vault selector with a decorative geometric strategy seal, followed by a calm asset ledger and contract parameters. The seal is identity art and encodes no live holdings, hedge, or status. Show only supported vaults, default to the current Strategy 01 vault, and keep raw contract addresses as explorer references rather than editable destinations. Put total assets and exit liquidity first; separate onchain holdings from the manager-marked perp book, show report freshness, and leave unavailable reads as unavailable rather than zero.

## Interaction rules

1. **Minimize end-user clicks.** Put the next sensible action in context, preserve entered values when recoverable, and avoid extra confirmation screens that add no understanding. Keep required wallet approval and transaction signatures distinct. Provide review when it helps users understand amounts, fees, destination, or risk.
2. **Every action has a feedback loop.** Show an immediate response to the click, then distinguish preparing, wallet confirmation, submitted transaction, onchain confirmation, failure, and recoverable next steps where applicable. Never use a generic “done” state for an unconfirmed transaction.
3. **Place feedback beside the action.** A persistent, readable result card is better than a distant line or a toast that disappears. Name the completed action, give the outcome in the user's units, and keep the transaction link available. Keep error text and retry path near the control.
4. **Motion reinforces state.** Use a brief entrance or check animation after a real success and modest transitions for pending stages. Never animate a balance, payout, or hedge through a misleading intermediate value. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; text, color, and shape must still communicate the state.
5. **No false certainty.** Show `—` or an unavailable explanation for failed reads. A button must not be enabled from a guessed allowance, balance, quote, or redemption limit. If a receipt reverts, show failure, never success.
6. **Make recovery obvious.** Preserve the entered amount after a failed wallet or chain action. After success, update position data and reset only the completed input. The current vault has no redemption queue; show available idle-USDC liquidity and explain when an admin unwind is needed for the remainder.
7. **Keep accessibility native.** Use semantic buttons, labels, `role="status"` for progress and success, `role="alert"` for failure, keyboard-visible focus, sufficient hit areas, and readable results with reduced motion.

## Wallet entry and funding

- Use **Get started** before an account is selected. Present **Continue with passkey** and **Connect existing wallet** as equal, clearly named choices. A new passkey opens a new EOA address and does not move an existing position.
- The wallet popup uses the **Counterweight access study**: a wide cream-and-plum split case with one illustrative, deliberately uneven sculpture and stable action cards. Carry this shell through choice, passkey, connected account, recovery phrase, pending, and error states. The separate RainbowKit wallet selector and account controls use the same palette, type, squared edges, and shadow. The sculpture is identity art, never a live balance, wallet verification, or transaction status. On phones it becomes a short header so the actual controls remain visible. Keep recovery text and the phrase on calm, readable surfaces; pause decorative motion during account actions and honor reduced motion.
- Label the active address **Passkey wallet** or **Connected wallet**. Ask for passkey verification when an approval, deposit, authorization, or exit requires a signature; keep approval and deposit as separate onchain states.
- For an empty passkey wallet, show the receiving address and QR code with the selected chain, exact USDC token contract, and required native gas. Keep the funding instructions next to the deposit action, and refresh balances without losing the selected route.
- Offer recovery phrase export from the account menu only after a fresh passkey check. Explain that the phrase controls the funds, keep it visible only while requested, and never store it in app persistence.
- Keep the deposit action focused on the Entry Ticket. Put detailed vault composition with the real-position measurements, available on demand rather than beside the deposit form.
- Keep `/deposit-concepts` development-only. It compares the Deposit Press and Counterweight Sculpture as isolated design studies with fictional balances and manual transaction states. The selected concept, local appearance, and phone preview must not change the live page or saved theme. The Deposit Press has been selected for the home page; keep the Counterweight Sculpture as a comparison only. The preview and live page share presentation components, while transaction state remains separate. In the press study, keep the machine beside tracking controls. Each confirmed milestone feeds one new strip of the paper record; pending steps rotate the wheel and roller without advancing the paper. Preserve the partial record on failure or pause. The final share confirmation finishes and stamps the receipt and autoplays the shared staking/short illustration. Keep that illustration distinct from deposit milestones, with the separate-allocation explanation and replay controls. Move the progress underline continuously between steps, use short directional content transitions, and animate the ticket height while preserving source and amount on back navigation. Exiting controls must be inert. Receipt text is immediately available regardless of motion; reduced motion shows settled artwork and immediate transitions, and hidden/offscreen animation pauses.
- Keep `/deposit-lab` development-only. It is a fully labeled simulation for walking the experience, never a source of real wallet, quote, or transaction state. Never use its sample numbers in the public flow.

## Review checklist for any user action

- Can the user tell immediately that the click registered?
- Can they distinguish wallet approval from onchain confirmation?
- Is success tied to a verified receipt or application state?
- Is the result prominent, specific, persistent, and close to the control?
- Are failure and recovery clear without losing useful input?
- Does the path use the fewest meaningful steps?
- Are dark/light, mobile/desktop, keyboard, and reduced-motion states readable?
