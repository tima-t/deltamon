Research and design notes, 29 September 2026. This is a product design record, not public copy.

## Existing flow audit

The former deposit panel was a generic bordered card with a separate static route image. The disconnected cross-chain state said “Choose a source” before any source was visible, and the direct button said “Review deposit” while it actually opened wallet entry. Review, pending, success, and failure were visually small relative to the amount control. The nearby composition card looked like a dashboard widget from another design system.

The live wallet path was inspected in the browser. A new development-only simulation at `/deposit-lab` was then walked through from connection to source, amount, review, wallet authorization, submitted transfer, and confirmed shares. I also simulated approval failure and verified that the amount remained and the failed step could be retried. The simulation never connects a wallet or submits a transaction. It returns 404 outside development.

## Design decision

The deposit chapter is an **entry ticket** with a stable progress rail and one active decision. The adjacent composition ledger was removed because it competed with the deposit task. The permanent editorial title and static three-node route were subsequently removed as well: the compact ticket registration identifies the deposit, source, and destination, while the active step uses the full available chapter width at every breakpoint. Review contains the share ownership and separate-allocation explanation; Track contains the detailed animated route. Wide receipts use a single row, returning to two or one columns on smaller screens. The lab matches this layout, with simulation controls available in a collapsed disclosure above the ticket. The detailed asset mix remains available as a disclosure in the real-position chapter. A print-like visual language makes the site distinctive; operational status stays explicit. No character art is used as a transaction indicator.

The real flow retains its existing chain and vault checks. The compact registration rail updates to the selected source chain. Review names both input and estimated shares, and success requires an onchain receipt. Cross-chain progress remains a separate sequence with recovery actions. The mock is labeled as a simulation at every level and is development-only.

## Follow-up boundary

The simulation validates the UI sequence and recovery copy. It does not prove a live deposit succeeds. A real funded wallet and deployed vault are required for a full integration test. Never substitute mock amounts for production balance, allowance, quote, or receipt data.

## Cross-chain motion study, 30 September 2026

The transfer tracker now treats the Entry Ticket as a printed transit map. Four fixed stations are connected by curved lines. Confirmed legs draw in sage, the active leg carries a slow violet signal, and a failure stops the signal. The adjacent status cards retain exact operational language. The signal is indeterminate: elapsed time and final share quantity are not inferred from its position.

The visual reference was [Codrops' survey of SVG motion paths](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/12/03/motion-paths-past-present-and-future/); [Motion's SVG path animation](https://motion.dev/docs/animate) supports drawing a leg at a state change. [Adobe's progress bar guidance](https://react-spectrum.adobe.com/v3/ProgressBar.html) informed the choice to avoid an invented completion percentage when duration is unknown. [Stripe's asynchronous payment state discussion](https://stripe.com/blog/payment-api-design) reinforced that a submitted route and a final result are separate states. The reduced-motion version follows [MDN's `prefers-reduced-motion` reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/%40media/prefers-reduced-motion): all stations, labels, and completed paths remain visible with travel animation disabled.

`/deposit-lab` now has manual source-sent, routing, arrival, vault-call, mint-check, confirmed, failed, and expired positions. These use the same tracker as the live panel, but their status and amount are explicitly fictional and never call a wallet, Aurora, or a chain. A reviewer can jump between positions or advance through the happy path to compare motion and copy.

## Source constellation, 30 September 2026

The source chooser is now a small observatory plate within the Entry Ticket. Its stellar cores are proportional in area to the known, positive USDC balances; source names, formatted balances, and approximate shares remain printed alongside. The concept borrows the tactile response of [Unseen Studio's organic particle experiment](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/09/11/when-cells-collide-the-making-of-an-organic-particle-experiment-with-rapier-three-js/) and the depth of [ExoSky's interactive starfield](https://brennenhill.com/projects/exosky/) without making transaction controls chase moving points. [Observable Plot's radius-scale guidance](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/scales) informed the square-root sizing so circle area reflects value. The one-click source handoff uses [Motion shared layout](https://motion.dev/docs/react-layout-animations); [W3C motion guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions) informed the pause and reduced-motion behavior.

The visual is a selector, not a transfer or balance guarantee. Unreadable balances get a literal unavailable row, not a sized star. A fundable zero-balance Monad wallet gets a hollow marker. The development-only lab uses fictional 2.24 / 2.42 / 0.31 USDC balances to inspect sizing and the amount-step transition.

## Strategy branch and transfer motion, 6 October 2026

The tracker now makes the vault strategy visible without adding a misleading dependency to the deposit sequence. `DeltaMonVault.deposit` receives USDC and mints shares together. Staking and short management occur separately, so allocation is an explanatory branch from the vault rather than a fifth numbered milestone before shares. “Put funds to work” pairs “Buy & stake MON” with “Increase the MON short,” in the same violet/amber language as the Exposure Plate. The visible illustration label and separate-allocation copy persist through pending, failed, and confirmed states. No allocation completion or per-deposit attribution is inferred from the share receipt.

I researched [Codrops' cinematic SVG map study](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/21/creating-scroll-driven-svg-map-animations-with-gsap/) for the combination of a traveling marker and path drawing, and [its SVG path tutorial](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2022/01/19/animate-anything-along-an-svg-path/) for restrained trails. [Motion's SVG documentation](https://motion.dev/docs/react-svg-animation) supports receipt drawing in the existing animation library. The selected treatment keeps the map still: no camera pans or scroll-controlled transaction progress. A single violet carrier travels only along the active deposit leg over 2.8 seconds; a confirmed path draws in 650ms, and the station check settles in 300ms. An unsuccessful leg loses the moving signal immediately. Historical confirmed states start settled.

The live tracker automatically runs a finite 3.6-second illustration once a deposit initiated in the current visit has a verified share mint. A session key prevents repeat plays during status polling; restored receipts do not autoplay. Position refresh happens immediately on confirmation, while scrolling to the return route waits for the illustration to end. Reduced motion bypasses playback and continues immediately. The lab keeps “Play strategy” as its default, with an optional “Preview live autoplay” control; live users can replay the illustration afterward. A violet pulse starts at the vault node and travels down the connector for 750ms. The two colored branch pulses begin at 650ms and 770ms, overlapping the handoff slightly; only as they reach the legs do the MON discs assemble and the short illustration trace in. One shared replay state synchronizes the connector, fork, and leg artwork. The fixed station labels have a plum stroke behind the type so the connector never harms readability. It ends in the same explanatory state with no success ticks, invented amounts, or “allocation complete” text. Pause/resume freezes ambient and explanatory motion. [Motion's reduced-motion hook](https://motion.dev/docs/react-use-reduced-motion) and [page-visibility hook](https://motion.dev/docs/react-use-page-in-view) inform accessibility and hidden-tab behavior; the viewport observer pauses animation when the map leaves view. Text and status remain available without motion. The route geometry measures its actual width so station numbers and labels retain their size on phones.

The development lab adds local diagram appearance and reduced-motion previews, without changing the visitor's saved theme or OS preference. Browser verification covered 320px and 390px layouts, dark appearance, reduced motion, confirmed shares, active routing, failure, and replay pause/resume. The existing 24 cross-chain tests, TypeScript, component lint, and the production build passed. The build retains the dependency warning from `ox`'s dynamic import.

The full-width revision was reviewed in the live access screen and simulated source, amount, review, and confirmed Track screens. At desktop width, the four receipt cards share one row; 390px and 320px review layouts retain readable copy and have no horizontal page overflow. TypeScript, component lint, and diff whitespace checks passed.

The full-width animation correction gives the strategy fork the same measured width as the main map, with a 40px SVG matching its rendered height. The previous fixed 640×56 fork stretched horizontally and vertically while using non-scaling strokes; its normalized dash animation no longer matched the displayed curve. The fork now draws in native coordinates and uses ordinary strokes. See the [SVG painting specification](https://svgwg.org/svg2-draft/painting.html) and the [SVG working group’s non-scaling dash discussion](https://github.com/w3c/svgwg/issues/323) for the coordinate-space interaction. Captured replay frames show both colored pulses reaching the ends of their routes.
